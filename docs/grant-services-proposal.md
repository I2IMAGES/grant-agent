## Part I — The Opportunity

### 1.1 The Problem We Solve

Transportation is the invisible barrier in human services. A client can be referred to substance abuse treatment, scheduled for a medical appointment, enrolled in a job training program, or assigned a caseworker — and still not show up because they have no way to get there. The research is consistent:

- **25% of low-income patients** miss or delay medical care due to transportation (National Academy of Sciences, 2019)
- **Transportation barriers** account for 3.6 million Americans missing or delaying medical care annually
- In Maricopa County, an estimated **40% of behavioral health no-shows** are transportation-related
- NMET services reduce emergency department utilization by an average of **17%** among Medicaid populations

Inward2Onward fills this gap. We transport clients to appointments, treatment programs, job training, social services, and community resources — closing the loop between service referral and actual service delivery.

### 1.2 Why Funders Are Paying Attention Now

The federal funding landscape has shifted dramatically in favor of organizations like Inward2Onward:

- **SAMHSA**, **HRSA**, **ACL**, and **HUD** are all issuing NOFOs in 2026 that explicitly require transportation components in their funded programs
- **Small Business Administration** and **MBDA** programs are prioritizing minority-owned and women-owned firms in underserved communities
- **HUBZone** designation creates a federal contracting advantage with agencies required to set aside contracts for HUBZone firms

The daily grant discovery agent is identifying 6-10 actionable opportunities per day across these funding streams. The bottleneck is no longer finding grants — it is converting them into submitted applications.

## Part II — The Seliger Framework Applied

Jake Seliger and Associates built one of the most cited grant-writing practices in the country on a deceptively simple model: **the funder has a problem; you are the solution.** Every section of a competitive grant application answers one of five questions.

### The Five Questions Every Application Must Answer

| Section | The Question | What Funders Are Really Asking |
|---------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Needs Statement** | Why does this problem exist here, now? | Is the problem real and documented? |
| **Program Design** | What exactly will you do about it? | Is the intervention specific and plausible? |
| **Evaluation Plan** | How will you know it worked? | Can you measure outcomes, not just activities? |
| **Organizational Capacity** | Why are YOU the right organization? | Have you done this before? |
| **Budget Narrative** | How will the money be spent? | Is this cost-effective and honest? |

Every grant application produced under this engagement follows this structure — adapted to the specific funder's language and priorities.

---

### 2.1 Needs Statement (The "Why Here, Why Now")

**Standard approach for Inward2Onward applications:**

The needs statement for every application will anchor on three data layers:

1. **National data** — cite federal studies on transportation barriers in healthcare, employment, and social services access
2. **Arizona/Maricopa County data** — AHCCCS ridership gaps, Maricopa County social determinants of health reports, Arizona Commerce Authority workforce data
3. **Inward2Onward client data** — ride volume, no-show rescue rates, referred populations, zip codes served

The needs statement ends with a **gap statement**: *Inward2Onward currently serves X clients per month. Funding from [funder] would allow us to serve Y additional clients in [target population] who currently have no transportation option.*

**For PARTNERSHIP applications** (submitted on behalf of a nonprofit partner), the needs statement focuses on the nonprofit's client population and how transportation gaps undermine program outcomes. I2O provides the transportation data layer.

---

### 2.2 Program Design (The "Exactly What" Section)

This is where most small business grant applications lose. Funders fund programs, not businesses. The program design section must describe:

**For Direct Grant Applications:**
- Target population (who specifically — AHCCCS clients, workforce development participants, behavioral health clients)
- Service area (Glendale, Phoenix metro, Maricopa County — mapped to funder's priority geography)
- Specific activities (number of rides, days/hours of operation, dispatch model, vehicle capacity)
- Timeline (month-by-month implementation milestones)
- Partnerships (which referring agencies, healthcare providers, nonprofits are already engaged)

**For PARTNERSHIP Applications:**
- The nonprofit's program model (lead applicant)
- Inward2Onward's specific subcontract scope (rides per month, populations served, cost per ride)
- Integration with case management (how transportation is triggered, documented, and billed)
- Data sharing protocols (HIPAA compliance, ride confirmation, outcome tracking)

**NMET-Specific Program Elements to include in every design:**
- Scheduling protocol (minimum 24-hour advance notice vs. same-day STAT rides)
- Driver qualifications (background check, CPR/First Aid, defensive driving)
- Vehicle standards (ADA accessible options, GPS tracking, temperature-controlled)
- No-show intervention (reminder calls, reconfirmation system)
- Documentation (ride logs, trip confirmation, billing reconciliation)

---

### 2.3 Evaluation Plan (The "How We Know It Worked" Section)

Seliger's rule: **measure outcomes, not activities.** Funders see thousands of applications that promise to "provide rides." Few promise to measure what those rides produce.

**Standard outcome metrics for Inward2Onward:**

| Outcome | Measurement Method | Reporting Frequency |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Appointment kept rate | Ride completion logs vs. referral records | Monthly |
| No-show reduction | Baseline vs. program period comparison | Quarterly |
| Client satisfaction | Post-ride SMS survey (5-question) | Monthly |
| Referral partner satisfaction | Quarterly agency survey | Quarterly |
| Cost per completed ride | Budget actuals / completed rides | Monthly |
| Target population served | Demographic tracking by zip, income, diagnosis | Monthly |

**For behavioral health / substance abuse programs:**
- Treatment session attendance rate (transportation clients vs. control)
- 30/60/90-day retention in treatment
- Relapse event correlation with transportation access

**For workforce development programs:**
- Training session attendance rate
- Program completion rate
- Employment placement rate at 90 days

These metrics are built into every application's logic model and referenced in the budget narrative to justify cost-per-outcome.

### 2.4 Organizational Capacity (The "Why Us" Section)

This section is Inward2Onward's strongest asset. The answer to "why you" is threefold:

**Certifications** — WOSB, EDWOSB, HUBZone, minority-owned. These are not decorative — they are legal qualifications that give funders compliance credit for funding I2O.

**Operational track record** — document:
- Total rides completed to date
- Referring agency relationships (list them by name)
- On-time performance rate
- Vehicle fleet and ADA compliance
- Dispatch and scheduling infrastructure
- Insurance coverage and liability history

**Community rootedness** — Inward2Onward is based in Glendale, AZ and serves the communities where it operates. This matters to local and state funders especially. Include:
- Years in operation in the service area
- Clients from underserved zip codes (85301, 85302, etc.)
- Any existing MOU or partnership agreements with nonprofits, FQHCs, or county agencies

**Leadership narrative** — brief bio of EJ emphasizing lived connection to the community, business acumen, and certifications held. Funders fund people, not just organizations.

---

### 2.5 Budget Narrative (The "Show Your Math" Section)

Seliger's principle: **an honest budget tells a story.** Every line item should be justifiable in a single sentence. Funders have seen padded budgets. A lean, well-documented budget wins trust.

**Standard budget categories for Inward2Onward grants:**

| Category | Typical Items | Justification Standard |
|----------|--------------|----------------------|
| Personnel | Driver wages, dispatch coordinator, grant administrator (% FTE) | Market rate documentation, % time on program |
| Transportation Operations | Fuel, vehicle maintenance, insurance | Cost-per-mile actuals, fleet size |
| Technology | Dispatch software, GPS, ride tracking | Per-seat or per-unit pricing |
| Training | Driver certifications, CPR, HIPAA | Third-party training cost documentation |
| Indirect / Admin | Up to 15% of direct costs | Organization's established indirect rate |
| Evaluation | Survey tools, data reporting | Specific line item with vendor or staff time |

**Cost-per-ride benchmarking:** The industry average for NMET is $28–$45 per one-way trip depending on distance and vehicle type. I2O's rate should be documented against this benchmark in every budget narrative to demonstrate cost-effectiveness.

#### Core Service 2 — NICRA Establishment & Indirect Cost Strategy

**What it is:**
A Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement (NICRA) is the document your organization files with its cognizant federal agency to establish an official indirect cost rate — the percentage of direct costs you are permitted to recover for administrative overhead on federal awards. Without a NICRA, federal applicants are limited to the **10% de minimis rate** (or whatever the NOFO allows), often leaving significant overhead unrecovered. With a properly filed NICRA, rates of 20–40% are common for small transportation operators.

**What Inward2Onward delivers:**

- **Indirect cost rate analysis** — reviews your actual cost structure (administrative staff time, facility, insurance, technology, vehicle depreciation) against your direct program expenses and calculates a defensible proposed rate
- **Rate election decision memo** — a written recommendation on whether to pursue a NICRA with your cognizant federal agency or elect the 10% de minimis rate, with financial impact modeled over a 12-month grant period at three award sizes ($100K, $250K, $500K)
- **NICRA preparation package** — organizes your financial data into the format required by your cognizant federal agency (typically HHS Division of Cost Allocation for social service providers), including a cost allocation methodology narrative and the rate proposal form
- **Submission guidance** — step-by-step instructions for submitting to the correct federal office, expected timeline (typically 6–9 months for first-time applicants), and how to claim the de minimis rate in the interim
- **Budget narrative language** — standardized text that correctly references your NICRA or de minimis election in any grant application budget section

**Who needs this:**
Any NMET operator planning to apply for two or more federal grants totaling $200K+ in a 12-month period. One approved NICRA applies to all federal awards going forward.

**Deliverable:** Rate analysis, election memo, preparation package, and budget narrative language.  
**Timeline:** 15 business days.  
**Investment: $2,400**

*Note: NICRA submission to the federal agency and negotiation is a separate process handled directly between your organization and the cognizant agency. I2O prepares the package; the operator files it.*

#### Core Service 3 — Rate Card Development & Market Benchmarking

**What it is:**
The single most important document for the PARTNERSHIP lane and for every federal contract bid: a written rate card that documents your actual cost per ride against published industry benchmarks. Without it, budget reviewers have no basis to evaluate your pricing. With it, you become the lowest-cost, most defensible option in the room.

**What Inward2Onward delivers:**

- **Operational cost-per-ride calculation** — works from your actual fuel, driver labor, vehicle depreciation, insurance, and dispatch costs to calculate your all-in cost per one-way trip by vehicle type (standard sedan, wheelchair-accessible van, stretcher van) and distance band (0–5 miles, 5–15 miles, 15–30 miles, 30+ miles)
- **Industry benchmark comparison** — compares your rates against:
  - Medicaid NEMT broker reimbursement rates in Arizona (AHCCCS published rates)
  - National NMET average ($28–$45 per one-way trip, varying by vehicle class)
  - Lyft/Uber Health and similar network rates for non-accessible trips

- **Published rate card document** — a clean, one-page rate card formatted for attachment to grant applications, subcontract proposals, and nonprofit partnership outreach materials
- **Budget narrative justification** — a 200-word paragraph that correctly references the benchmark comparison to justify your rates in any federal grant budget
- **Competitive positioning summary** — a one-paragraph statement, ready to paste into any grant application's Organizational Capacity section, explaining your cost advantage relative to competitors

**Who needs this:**
Any NMET operator pursuing PARTNERSHIP subcontracts, federal contracts, or any grant requiring a detailed budget narrative. Required for VA, CMS, DOT, and most managed care subcontracts.

**Deliverable:** Cost analysis model, rate card document, benchmark comparison, and budget narrative language.
